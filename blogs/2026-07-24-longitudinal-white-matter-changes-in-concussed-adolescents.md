Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is a leading cause of death and long-term disability in children, with many experiencing persistent symptoms even after mild TBIs. Exposure to adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) can have physiological effects that may alter how the brain responds to injury, yet the effects of ACEs on white matter injury and recovery processes remain unclear. This study examined whether a history of ACEs is associated with patterns of longitudinal change in white matter microstructure in children with mTBI.
