Background: The biological mechanism linking rainfall with leptospirosis transmission is well established, but rigorous quantitative evidence for the insular Caribbean remains scarce, limited mostly to descriptive reports of post-hurricane outbreaks. We aimed to quantify the association between rainfall and leptospirosis incidence in the Dominican Republic, where leptospirosis is endemic, between 2012 and 2026 using a distributed-lag approach. Methods: We conducted an ecological time-series study complemented by a province panel with fixed effects.
