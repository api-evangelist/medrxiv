For over a decade, the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs) have served as the principal framework for quantitative modeling of the socioeconomic dimensions of global environmental change. The SSP scenarios describe many of the ecological and social processes thought to shape pandemic risk, including the emergence of novel pathogens (accelerated by processes such as deforestation, livestock intensification, and land-use change) and their subsequent spread (mediated by factors such as inequality, human mobility, and health system capacity).
