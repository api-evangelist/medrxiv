Cervical cancer is a leading cause of cancer death among women in Zambia, and vaccination of girls aged 9-14 years against human papillomavirus (HPV) is the principal primary prevention strategy. Because the vaccine is given to minors, uptake depends on parental consent, which is shaped by how parents perceive the vaccine. In Kabompo, a rural, ethnically diverse district of North-Western Province, only 59% of eligible girls had been vaccinated by 2024, well below the global target, yet no qualitative evidence existed on how parents in such settings understand and decide about the vaccine.
