Patients with history of neck radiation for Human Papilloma Virus (HPV)-associated head and neck cancer experience rapid progression of carotid artery stenosis. The present study sought to determine whether screening and treating asymptomatic carotid artery stenosis in patients with a history of neck radiation is cost-effective. This study is a cost-utility analysis using a probabilistic Markov model over a thirty-year time horizon assessing carotid screening and treatment to avoid neurologic consequences of neck radiation for HPV-associated head and neck cancer.
