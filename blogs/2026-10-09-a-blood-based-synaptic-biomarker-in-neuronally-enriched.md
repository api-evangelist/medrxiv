Progressive multiple sclerosis (PMS) lacks blood-based biomarkers of the neurodegenerative processes driving disability. We measured synaptopodin, a postsynaptic protein, in neuronal-enriched extracellular vesicles (NEVs) across two independent cohorts: the SPRINT-MS trial (n=184) and a real-world validation cohort (n=268). A one SD increase in baseline synaptopodin was associated with ~32% lower risk of confirmed disability progression in PMS (p=0.011), with the strongest effect in secondary PMS (SPMS) (~57% lower odds, p=0.019).
